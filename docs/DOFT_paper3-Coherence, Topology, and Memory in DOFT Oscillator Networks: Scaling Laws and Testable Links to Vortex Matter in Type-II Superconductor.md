# Paper 3 — Draft v0.6 (DOFT-only scaling + superconductors bridge)

## Title
**Coherence, Topology, and Memory in DOFT Oscillator Networks: Scaling Laws and Testable Links to Vortex Matter in Type-II Superconductors**

## Author
**C. Agostino** — Independent Researcher, Buenos Aires, Argentina

## Abstract
Delayed-Oscillator Field Theory (DOFT) is a speculative framework in which stable structure emerges from networks of coupled oscillators with finite delay and internal memory. In prior work we reported (i) prime-space “fingerprints” generated by a delayed-locking grammar plus a minimal correction law, and (ii) integer-participation structure under structural noise via a mother-frequency observable—both extracted from superconducting materials datasets. Here we shift from phenomenological mappings to a **DOFT-only** simulation program designed to probe how coherent structure scales with network size **N** and topology.

We introduce a multi-wave pipeline (Ola1–Ola4) that **separates candidate search from dynamical measurement**: the Explorer proposes and filters candidate network instances to manage combinatorial growth, while the Sweep stage integrates the governing coupled differential dynamics and computes late-time observables. Three robust findings organize the results. **(1)** Mean lock coherence decreases monotonically with network size, consistent over the explored range with a geometric decay close to **R ∝ N^(−1/3)** (and also compatible with exponential relaxation toward a finite floor). **(2)** Survival at larger N correlates with high participation of a distinct memory layer (S2), suggesting memory acts as stability overhead under geometric stress. **(3)** Topology becomes increasingly selective with N, including an apparent **monolithic ceiling** around **N ≈ 8–9** for single-module templates where survival collapses.

These scaling patterns parallel known phenomenology of **vortex matter** in type-II superconductors, where increasing vortex density drives a transition from ordered Abrikosov solid to disordered vortex phases. We propose a **falsifiable bridge**: DOFT’s coherence observable may behave like a correlation-length proxy in a disordered elastic medium, making the observed exponent (~1/3) plausibly comparable to collective-pinning scaling regimes reported in the vortex literature. We emphasize this correspondence as a *testable hypothesis*, not an identification.

**Keywords:** delayed oscillators, coherence scaling, topology selection, memory layer, structural ceiling, vortex lattice, collective pinning, Abrikosov lattice

## 1. Motivation and context (what Paper 3 adds)
DOFT inverts the usual modeling direction: instead of starting from microscopic Hamiltonians, it starts from **constraints**—delayed coupling, finite memory, and lock formation in oscillator networks—and asks what stable structure is permitted.

**Paper 1 (superconductors datasets):** introduced prime-space fingerprints generated by a delayed-locking grammar plus a minimal correction law, suggesting low-parameter constraints can produce discrete stable signatures in superconducting materials.

**Paper 2 (superconductors datasets):** studied integer-participation structure under structural noise using a mother-frequency observable, again emphasizing discrete structure and robustness to noise in the same material class.

**This Paper 3 (DOFT-only mechanism):** isolates the DOFT dynamics in a controlled simulation pipeline to answer a concrete question:

> **What is the intrinsic cost of increasing N in DOFT networks, and what mechanism allows coherent structure to persist as complexity increases?**

We deliberately use observables that are interpretation-light: coherence, selection-funnel counts/rates, topology conditioning, and a memory-participation metric (S2). In Discussion we propose a bridge to vortex phenomenology as a **reality-test interface** for the scaling claims.

## 2. Overview of the Ola program (Ola1 → Ola4)
The program proceeds in “waves” (Olas) designed to extend scale while preserving comparability.

- **Ola1 (context / optional appendix):** high-cost sweep that motivated a layered internal model and vocabulary (Q1/S1/S2; locks; memory participation). Ola1 is not required for the main claims, but it provides historical context and definitions.

- **Ola2:** template-driven candidate search for small networks (low N regime), followed by Sweep measurement.

- **Ola3:** extension to intermediate N. Filters tighten to control combinatorial growth; **observables still come only from Sweep**.

- **Ola4:** extension to larger N under stricter candidate filters and higher coherence thresholds, again measured by Sweep.

Across waves we preserve: **(i)** a shared template vocabulary, **(ii)** a consistent coherence observable R computed from Sweep outputs, **(iii)** a stable structural-class / taxonomy layer, and **(iv)** standardized exports (CSV/JSON summaries and manifests).

## 3. Definitions and observables

### 3.1 Coherence / lock strength R
We quantify global coherence using a lock-strength statistic **R ∈ [0,1]**, computed from late-time network behavior under **Sweep dynamics** (e.g., last-window mean). Larger R indicates stronger coherent locking across the network.

> **Important:** all quantitative R values in this paper are **Sweep outputs** (from differential dynamics integration). The Explorer does not “generate coherence”; it only selects which candidates the Sweep will evaluate.

### 3.2 Memory participation (S2)
We quantify “memory” as participation of an internal memory layer **S2**, reported as **S2-share** (normalized contribution of S2 in the stabilized structure). We treat S2-share as an operational DOFT observable (not an ontological claim).

### 3.3 Selection funnel and grades
Outcomes are summarized through a selection funnel applied to Sweep-measured observables:

- **evaluated:** simulated candidates measured by Sweep  
- **candidate:** passes minimal coherence threshold **R ≥ R_min** and viability checks  
- **promoted:** passes stricter criteria and is retained in the paper dataset  

Grades (A/B/…) provide a coarse evidence label. (We report sensitivity to grade filters in an appendix, because A-only vs A/B can shift counts without shifting the core trends.)

### 3.4 Topology, density, and structural classes
Each run is associated with:
- a **template topology** (ring/ladder/bipartite/complete/…),
- a connectivity measure (e.g., **edge density**),
- and a higher-level **structural class** (taxonomy/rollup) used for stability checks across waves.

### 3.5 Secondary diagnostics (supporting, not backbone)
We record optional diagnostics (participation entropy, phase variance, entropy-quality proxies, robustness scores). These are used mainly as sanity checks and appendix material.

## 4. Methods (Candidate Search + ODE Sweep Measurement)

### 4.1 Separation of responsibilities: Explorer vs Sweep
The pipeline explicitly separates candidate generation from dynamical measurement.

- **Explorer:** proposes and filters candidate network instances (templates + rehydrated structures) to manage the combinatorial search space. This is a search/selection front-end.

- **Sweep:** integrates the governing coupled differential dynamics (ODE / delayed-ODE depending on wave configuration) and computes late-time observables (R, S2-share, robustness/entropy proxies) using standardized windowing.

**All reported results come from Sweep outputs.** The Explorer only determines which candidates are evaluated.

### 4.2 Template generator and recursive rehydration
For each target N, we define template families (rings, ladders, bipartites, etc.). Higher waves may rehydrate lower-wave structures into larger candidates while preserving a minimal set of invariants (locking grammar constraints; memory compatibility).

### 4.3 Thresholds, tractability, and reporting discipline
Because the combinatorial upper bound grows rapidly with N, we tighten candidate filters and coherence cutoffs with N to keep evaluation tractable. Therefore:

- we always report **both counts and rates** through the funnel, and  
- we interpret “collapses” using **template-conditioned comparisons** (heatmaps) rather than raw totals alone, and  
- we include a **selection-sensitivity appendix** (common-threshold replot, grade filter comparisons).

Illustrative wave thresholds used in the current release:
- Ola2: **R_min ≈ 0.75**
- Ola3: **R_min ≈ 0.85**
- Ola4: **R_min ≈ 0.90**

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Figure 1 — Coherence decays with complexity (core scaling)
**Claim:** Mean coherence decreases monotonically with network size N.

Figure 1 plots coherence **R vs N** across waves, highlighting per-N averages. Over the explored range, the decay is well-approximated by a geometric scaling near:

> **R(N) ∝ N^(−1/3)**

and is also compatible with exponential relaxation toward a finite floor. The key empirical point is monotone decay: coherence becomes increasingly “expensive” with size.

**Interpretation (minimal):** As N increases, the fraction of the space that supports strong global locking shrinks.

**Interpretation (stronger but still internal):** The exponent near 1/3 suggests a geometric scaling relationship between effective coherence and oscillator count, consistent with an increasing mismatch burden as degrees of freedom grow.

---

### 5.2 Figure 2 — Memory participation (S2) as stability overhead (“metabolic cost”)
**Claim:** Survival at larger N correlates with higher S2 participation.

Figure 2 plots **S2-share vs N** for surviving/promoted outcomes. Surviving larger-N systems frequently exhibit high S2 participation (often clustering in the ~60–70% range in the explored sets), indicating that persistence at higher complexity is not achieved by geometric alignment alone.

**Interpretation (S2 as overhead / metabolic cost):**  
Small-N systems can stabilize primarily through direct geometric alignment (S1-dominant locking). In contrast, surviving large-N systems require high S2 participation, suggesting memory functions as a stability buffer—an overhead channel that compensates for geometric stress when perfect global alignment is no longer achievable.

> This is not “intelligence”; it is an **overhead cost** paid to preserve coherence when geometry alone fails.

---

### 5.3 Figure 4 — Evidence for the S2 mechanism (S2-share vs R)
To support the mechanism more directly, Figure 4 plots **S2-share vs R** from Sweep outputs (color by N or wave).

**What we want to see (and what would falsify it):**
- If memory is genuinely acting as stability overhead, then among survivors—especially at larger N—higher S2-share should correlate with maintaining higher coherence (or preventing collapse).
- If the scatter is flat or inverted when conditioning on N, then “S2 helps stability” becomes weaker (or wrong), and the story must be revised.

This figure is intentionally *mechanistic*: it tests whether S2 participates in sustaining coherence rather than merely appearing alongside it.

---

### 5.4 Figure 3 — Template×N heatmap (topology selectivity + ceiling)
**Claim A (Topological divergence):** Topology becomes increasingly selective as N grows.  
**Claim B (Monolithic ceiling):** Single-module templates exhibit an apparent survival ceiling around **N ≈ 8–9**.

We present a **template×N heatmap** of promoted survival rate (main) with cell annotations (rate and n). This figure is critical because it reveals selectivity and collapse **without** over-parameterized modeling.

#### Topological divergence (degeneracy → selectivity)
At low N, lock formation appears topologically degenerate: multiple geometries can survive. At intermediate and larger N, topology becomes a primary determinant of survival: some templates remain viable while others collapse.

**Physical reading (internal):** geometry becomes a stronger constraint as the system grows; “shape matters more” when coherence is expensive.

#### Monolithic ceiling around N≈8–9
Beyond general needle-like survival at high N, the template-conditioned view suggests an additional structural limit: survival rates for monolithic single-module forms (notably simple rings and closely related monolithic patterns, depending on the template set) drop to near zero around N≈8–9.

**Interpretation:** beyond this ceiling, complexity likely cannot scale linearly within a single coherent module; survival requires **modularization** (multi-block / clustered organization) and/or additional stabilizing degrees of freedom.

---

### 5.5 Structural classes and taxonomic stability (bridge back to Papers 1–2)
Beyond raw rates, Sweep outcomes can be assigned to discrete **structural classes** (taxonomy/rollups). Across waves, class-level organization persists despite tightened thresholds and changing N ranges. This persistence suggests the DOFT dynamics organizes into discrete attractor-like classes rather than a smooth continuum.

This is the mechanistic echo of Papers 1–2: where those papers observed discrete structure in superconductors datasets, here we observe discrete structure arising *within the DOFT dynamics itself*.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Why “−1/3” is the right kind of claim (and what we do *not* claim)
We claim a robust monotone decay of coherence with N, and we report that within the explored range a power-law fit is close to −1/3 (also compatible with exponential-to-floor forms).

What we do **not** claim:
- that −1/3 is uniquely determined from first principles here,
- that it must hold indefinitely beyond the explored range,
- or that it implies a specific microscopic mechanism in real materials without additional tests.

The value of the exponent is that it makes the phenomenon **compressible** and **testable**.

---

### 6.2 Memory is not decoration: it is the scaling mechanism (or it isn’t)
The S2 result upgrades “memory” from vocabulary to a measurable scaling mechanism: large-N survival appears to require high S2 participation.

Figure 4 (S2-share vs R) is the core mechanistic test. If it supports the coupling, S2 becomes a credible stabilizing channel. If not, the memory story must be re-authored (and we accept that outcome).

---

### 6.3 Relation to Papers 1–2 (why this matters for your existing audience)
Your current audience is superconductivity-heavy. Paper 3 must “speak their language” **without** turning the paper into a Standard-Model proxy argument.

This paper’s role is:
- to show a compact **DOFT scaling law** (coherence vs complexity),
- to show an internal **stability mechanism** (memory overhead),
- to show a **structural transition** (topology selectivity + monolithic ceiling),
- and then to propose a **clean experimental interface** where those constraints can be tested (vortex matter).

## 6.4 Vortex matter as a physical testbed (speculative, but falsifiable)

### 6.4.1 Phenomenology (what solid-state readers recognize)
In type-II superconductors under applied magnetic field **H**, quantized flux penetrates as vortices. As field increases (vortex density increases), the system progresses through regimes often described as:
- ordered Abrikosov lattice (low disorder / low density),
- increasingly defective lattice / glassy regimes (intermediate),
- strong disorder / melting / liquid-like behavior (high density/temperature/material-dependent).

These regimes are routinely characterized by correlation lengths, Bragg peak widths, pinning strength, and transport signatures (including the peak effect).

### 6.4.2 A controlled mapping (presented as hypothesis)
We propose the following mapping as a **testable hypothesis**:

- **N (DOFT oscillator count)** ↔ **effective vortex density** (monotone with H)
- **R (DOFT coherence)** ↔ **correlation / coherence scale** in vortex matter (order parameter proxy or correlation length proxy, depending on the experimental observable)
- **S2 (memory participation)** ↔ **disorder/pinning overhead** required to sustain residual coherence
- **template selectivity** ↔ **geometry/constraints becoming non-degenerate** at higher density
- **monolithic ceiling** ↔ **loss of viability of single-domain coherence** beyond a critical density

This mapping is intentionally conservative: it does *not* assert a one-to-one identification of mechanisms, only a *structural analogy with measurable predictions*.

---

### 6.4.3 The Larkin–Ovchinnikov bridge (what we can responsibly say)
Collective pinning theory (Larkin–Ovchinnikov) predicts that a correlated domain size / correlation length decreases with increasing field in disordered vortex matter:
> **R_c ∝ H^(−α)**

Reported α depends on dimensionality, disorder geometry, and regime (and is typically discussed in reviews such as Blatter et al.). Many layered or quasi-2D contexts report α in the **~0.3–0.4** neighborhood (regime-dependent), which overlaps numerically with **1/3**.

**What we can responsibly claim now:**
- DOFT produces a coherence-vs-complexity scaling close to −1/3 over the explored range.
- LO-type frameworks produce correlation-length-vs-field scaling with exponents that can be of similar magnitude in certain regimes.
- Therefore, **it is plausible** (not proven) that DOFT’s R behaves like a correlation-length proxy under an H↔N monotone mapping, and that the exponent overlap is an empirical target.

**What we should not claim (yet):**
- “R_network equals R_c” as a fact.
- “α matches YBCO/BSCCO” as a universal statement across conditions.
- direct material-specific parameter identification without additional calibration.

We present this as a **bridge hypothesis**: strong enough to motivate experiments, but framed so it can fail cleanly.

---

### 6.4.4 Concrete, falsifiable predictions (what a vortex-matter reader can test)
1. **Scaling law interface:** In a chosen regime (material + temperature window + disorder condition), a correlation-length proxy should show a power-law decay with field. The test is whether an exponent near ~1) is observed and whether ~1/3 is plausible in that regime.

2. **Ceiling interpretation:** The DOFT “monolithic ceiling” (N≈8–9) suggests a **sharp structural transition** where single-module coherence fails. The vortex-matter analogue is a transition where correlation length approaches a microscopic scale (vortex spacing or pinning length), beyond which long-range order is not viable.

3. **Pinning overhead:** If S2 is analogous to pinning overhead, then increasing density/field should increase the role of disorder-mediated stabilization in the regimes where residual coherence persists (testable via transport and imaging signatures that diagnose pinning dominance).

4. **Topology becomes selective at high density:** In anisotropic/layered materials, geometry constraints become more decisive at higher field; if the analogy holds, high-field regimes should exhibit stronger sensitivity to symmetry/constraints than low-field regimes.

---

## 6.5 Limitations and controls (what must be explicit)
- **Threshold tightening is a confound**: R_min increases with wave. That is why we must show (i) rates and (ii) template-conditioned heatmaps, and ideally (iii) a common-threshold sensitivity replot (Appendix E).

- **Finite template vocabulary**: “Topology selectivity” is defined relative to the template set. Expanding templates can change which topologies look privileged, but the *existence of selectivity growth with N* is the claim.

- **N range is limited (current runs)**: We report within the explored range. We avoid claiming asymptotic behavior.

- **Mapping to superconductors is a hypothesis**: we keep it test-anchored, not identity-anchored.

## 7. Conclusion
Using a DOFT-only candidate-search + ODE-sweep measurement program (Ola1–Ola4), we report:

1. **Coherence decays monotonically with network size**, consistent in the explored range with a geometric scaling near **R ∝ N^(−1/3)** (also compatible with exponential-to-floor forms).

2. **Surviving large-N systems exhibit high memory participation (S2)**, consistent with memory acting as **stability overhead** (a “metabolic cost” of coherence under geometric stress).

3. **Topology becomes increasingly selective with N**, including an apparent **monolithic ceiling** around **N≈8–9** for single-module templates, suggesting modularization as a survival requirement beyond a structural limit.

We then propose (as a falsifiable bridge) that these scaling behaviors provide a minimal structural lens for vortex matter in type-II superconductors, where increasing field/density reduces correlation scales and increases the role of pinning/disorder. The numerical proximity between the DOFT exponent (~1/3) and certain collective-pinning scaling regimes motivates targeted experimental comparison, but we frame this as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a resolved identification.
