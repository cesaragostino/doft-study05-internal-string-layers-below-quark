\title{Coherence Scaling in Hierarchical Oscillator Networks:\\Power-Law Decay, Memory Overhead, and Topological Constraints\\with Parallels to Disordered Condensed Matter}

\author{C. Agostino}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher, Buenos Aires, Argentina}
\email{cesar.agostino@gmail.com}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We study scaling laws for phase coherence in networks of coupled oscillators with internal memory, termed the Delayed Oscillator Field Theory (DOFT) framework. The model consists of hierarchically constructed networks: stable ``building blocks'' (single three-layer oscillators with well-defined entropic signatures) are composed into networks of size $N = 2$--$12$ across multiple topologies. Using a four-stage numerical pipeline that separates combinatorial search from rigorous dynamical integration, we establish three robust results.

First, mean global coherence $R$ is well-described by an effective scaling $R \propto N^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha = 0.32 \pm 0.03$; alternative functional forms (inverse, exponential) are not decisively excluded over this range but linear decay is ruled out. Second, memory-layer participation correlates weakly with coherence at fixed $N$, but the correlation shifts from negative at low $N$ to near-zero or weakly positive at high $N$, suggesting memory acts as stabilization overhead rather than a coherence driver. Third, single-domain topologies (rings) exhibit survival collapse at $N \approx 8$--$9$, while modular topologies (bipartites) persist to larger sizes with degraded but nonzero coherence.

The pipeline applies progressively stricter multi-dimensional filtering (coherence, entropy, phase stability); promotion rates collapse from 100\% at $N \leq 5$ to 0.7\% at $N = 9$--$12$, quantifying the ``topological ceiling'' in absolute terms.

These patterns exhibit suggestive parallels to correlation-length decay in disordered condensed matter systems, including vortex matter in type-II superconductors where Larkin--Ovchinnikov theory predicts $\xi \propto H^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha \sim 0.3$--$0.5$. We frame these correspondences as \emph{falsifiable hypotheses}. The DOFT framework and numerical data are publicly available; we encourage independent verification.

\textbf{Keywords:} coupled oscillators, coherence scaling, collective phenomena, delayed systems, topological constraints, correlation length
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Motivation: coherence scaling in complex systems}

How does collective order degrade as system complexity increases? This question arises across physics: from spin glasses to neural networks, from granular materials to biological oscillators. A common theme is that adding components incurs a ``coordination cost''---maintaining global coherence becomes progressively harder as the number of interacting elements grows.

Here we study this problem in a controlled computational setting: networks of coupled oscillators with internal memory, where we can systematically vary network size $N$, topology, and internal parameters. The central finding is a robust power-law decay of coherence with $N$, characterized by an exponent $\alpha \approx 1/3$.

This exponent turns out to have suggestive parallels to correlation-length scaling in disordered condensed matter systems, particularly vortex matter in type-II superconductors~\cite{Blatter1994}. We develop these parallels as testable hypotheses, not claimed identities.

\subsection{Context: prior work on discrete fingerprints}

This paper is the third in a series exploring delayed-oscillator phenomenology in the context of superconducting materials:

\textbf{Paper I}~\cite{Agostino2025a} introduced ``prime-space fingerprints'': discrete patterns generated by a locking grammar based on small primes $\{2,3,5,7\}$, combined with a universal two-parameter correction law. Applied to datasets of superconductors across multiple families (elemental, binary, cuprate, iron-based, high-pressure), the fingerprints reproduced discrete organizational features without per-material tuning.

\textbf{Paper II}~\cite{Agostino2025b} focused on ``integer participation'': using a mother-frequency observable $F_m = \Theta_D/T_c$ and a structural-noise correction, we showed that participation numbers cluster near integers with statistical significance far exceeding null models. The participation number $N$ scaled with experimental coherence length as $N \propto \xi_0^{0.43}$, bridging thermodynamic and spatial scales.

Both papers mapped external data (measured $T_c$, $\Theta_D$, $\xi_0$) onto a discrete framework. Neither addressed whether the framework's structure emerges intrinsically or requires empirical input.

\subsection{This work: intrinsic scaling from simulation}

Here we isolate the intrinsic dynamics by studying delayed-oscillator networks in a pure simulation environment, with no reference to superconducting datasets. The central questions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scaling:} How does global coherence degrade as network size $N$ increases? Is there a characteristic exponent?
\item \textbf{Mechanism:} What internal degree of freedom---if any---compensates for geometric failure at large $N$?
\item \textbf{Constraints:} Are all network topologies equally viable, or does complexity impose selection rules?
\end{enumerate}

The answers turn out to parallel vortex phenomenology in specific, quantitative ways. We present these parallels as hypotheses to test, not as claimed identities.

\subsection{Summary of main results}

Using a four-stage pipeline (Ola1--Ola4) that separates fast combinatorial search from rigorous ODE integration, we find:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Power-law coherence decay:} Mean coherence $R$ satisfies $R \propto N^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha = 0.32 \pm 0.03$ over $N = 2$--$12$. This exponent is consistent with $1/3$.

\item \textbf{Memory as overhead:} Memory-layer participation (S2-share) is weakly correlated with coherence at fixed $N$, but survivors at large $N$ exhibit systematically elevated S2. The S2--$R$ correlation shifts from negative at low $N$ to near-zero at high $N$.

\item \textbf{Topological ceiling:} Single-domain topologies (rings, ladders) show survival collapse at $N \approx 8$--$9$; modular topologies (bipartites) persist to larger $N$ with degraded but nonzero coherence.

\item \textbf{Critical heterogeneity:} Phase-variance dispersion peaks dramatically at $N = 6$--$7$, indicating coexistence of distinct dynamical regimes near the transition.
\end{enumerate}

These patterns map onto vortex phenomenology as shown in Table~\ref{tab:mapping}.

\section{Relation to Vortex Matter Physics}
%=============================================================================

Before describing the model and results in detail, we establish the physical context by reviewing vortex phenomenology and stating the proposed correspondences explicitly.

\subsection{Vortex lattice transitions: a brief review}

In type-II superconductors, magnetic flux penetrates as quantized vortices carrying flux $\Phi_0 = h/2e$. At low fields, repulsive vortex interactions favor an ordered Abrikosov lattice with hexagonal symmetry. As field increases:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Correlation length shrinks:} Disorder and thermal fluctuations reduce the scale over which the lattice maintains positional order. Neutron scattering (SANS) measures this via Bragg peak broadening; $\mu$SR probes local field variance.

\item \textbf{Lattice softening:} Elastic moduli decrease, enhancing susceptibility to pinning and fluctuations. This produces the ``Peak Effect''---an anomalous increase in critical current near $H_{c2}$~\cite{Paltiel2000}.

\item \textbf{Melting/glass transition:} At sufficiently high field or temperature, long-range order is lost entirely, yielding either a vortex liquid (clean limit) or vortex glass (disordered limit)~\cite{Blatter1994,Guillamon2009}.
\end{itemize}

Collective pinning theory~\cite{LarkinOvchinnikov1979,Feigelman1989} predicts that the correlation volume $V_c$ (over which displacements remain correlated) scales with pinning strength and dimensionality. In the weak collective pinning regime, correlation lengths satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\xi_{\parallel,\perp} \propto H^{-\alpha}, \quad \alpha \sim 0.3\text{--}0.5 \text{ (depends on $d$, anisotropy)}
\label{eq:larkin}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is smaller in quasi-2D layered systems.

\textbf{Crucial caveat:} These are \emph{hypotheses}, not identifications. The mapping is phenomenological---we propose that the mathematical structure of coherence loss in delayed-oscillator networks mirrors that in vortex systems, but we do not derive the mapping from microscopic principles.

\subsection{Why the exponent matters}

If the mapping $N \leftrightarrow H$ and $R \leftrightarrow \xi$ holds monotonically, then our finding $R \propto N^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha \approx 0.32$ suggests:
\begin{equation}
\xi(H) \propto H^{-\alpha}, \quad \alpha_{\mathrm{expected}} \approx 0.30\text{--}0.35
\label{eq:prediction}
\end{equation}
This is consistent with collective-pinning predictions for quasi-2D systems (Eq.~\ref{eq:larkin}) and with experimental measurements in BSCCO and YBCO near order-disorder transitions~\cite{Cubitt1993,Lee1993}.

\textbf{Falsification criterion:} If measurements in the intermediate-field regime yield $\alpha < 0.2$ or $\alpha > 0.45$ when extracted via comparable methodology (power-law fit over at least one decade), the correspondence fails.

\section{Model and Computational Methods}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The delayed-oscillator network}

Each network node $j = 1, \ldots, N$ is a three-layer oscillator with coordinates $(Q_1^{(j)}, S_1^{(j)}, S_2^{(j)})$ representing fast geometric, intermediate, and slow memory layers, respectively. The dynamics are governed by coupled delay-differential equations:
\begin{align}
\dot{Q}_1^{(j)} &= f_Q\bigl(Q_1^{(j)}, S_1^{(j)}, \{Q_1^{(k)}(t-\tau_{jk})\}_{k \in \mathcal{N}(j)}\bigr) \\
\dot{S}_1^{(j)} &= f_{S1}\bigl(Q_1^{(j)}, S_1^{(j)}, S_2^{(j)}\bigr) \\
\dot{S}_2^{(j)} &= f_{S2}\bigl(S_1^{(j)}, S_2^{(j)}\bigr)
\end{align}
where $\tau_{jk}$ are coupling delays, $\mathcal{N}(j)$ denotes neighbors of node $j$ in the network graph, and the functions $f$ encode nonlinear coupling (see Appendix~\ref{app:model} for explicit forms).

\textbf{Key features:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Finite delay:} Information propagates with latency $\tau$, preventing instantaneous global coordination.
\item \textbf{Memory layer (S2):} A slow degree of freedom that can stabilize dynamics on timescales longer than the fast geometric adjustment.
\item \textbf{Topology:} The network graph (ring, ladder, bipartite, complete) determines which nodes interact.
\end{itemize}

This architecture is \emph{not} derived from superconducting physics---it is a generic model of coupled oscillators with hierarchy and delay. Its relevance to vortex matter is proposed, not assumed.

\subsection{Observables}

\textbf{Global coherence $R$:} Measures phase synchronization across the network:
\begin{equation}
R = \left\langle \frac{1}{N}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N} e^{i\theta_j(t)}\right| \right\rangle_{\text{late time}}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_j$ is the phase of oscillator $j$ (extracted from the $Q_1$ layer) and averaging is over the final 20\% of the integration window. $R = 1$ indicates perfect lock; $R \to 0$ indicates incoherence.

\textbf{Lock score $L$:} Measures temporal convergence stability (R\_mean\_lastW in the pipeline). Used operationally to filter candidates but \emph{distinct from} $R$: Pearson correlation $r(L, R) = 0.004$ with 95\% CI $[-0.06, 0.07]$ ($n = 1187$), confirming statistical independence. $L$ ensures numerical tractability; $R$ quantifies the coordination cost.

\textbf{Memory participation (S2-share):} Normalized contribution of the memory layer:
\begin{equation}
\text{S2-share} = \frac{p_{S2}}{p_{S1} + p_{S2}}
\end{equation}
where $p_\ell$ is the mean participation of layer $\ell$. High S2-share indicates active memory engagement.

\textbf{Phase variance:} Internal spread of phases, $\sigma^2_\theta = \text{Var}[\theta_j(t)]$, averaged over the late-time window.

\textbf{Participation entropy $H_{\mathrm{part}}$:} Quantifies how broadly dynamical activity is distributed across internal layers. Defined as the normalized Shannon entropy of layer participation fractions; $H_{\mathrm{part}} = 0$ indicates activity concentrated in a single layer, while $H_{\mathrm{part}} = 1$ indicates uniform distribution. This measure is used in Appendix~\ref{app:disorder_proxy} to construct a roughness-like proxy.

\subsection{Computational parameters}

All integrations use: $T_{\mathrm{ticks}} = 20{,}000$, window $W = 4{,}000$, $dt = 0.0025$, 4 seeds per entity (results averaged over seeds). Coherence thresholds are applied at the Explorer candidate filter and at taxonomy/promotion. The sweep itself does not require a coherence threshold for completion or inclusion in reported statistics---it runs and reports metrics as long as primary observables are finite. Total computational time: approximately 10 days on dual 8-core systems.

\subsection{The Ola pipeline: multi-stage filtering}

Exhaustive exploration of network configurations is intractable. We address this via a four-stage recursive pipeline (``Ola1--Ola4''), where each stage applies \emph{multi-dimensional} filtering---entities must satisfy coherence, entropy, and phase-stability criteria simultaneously.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Ola1 (baseline):} Single-node (three-layer) oscillators. Defines operational vocabulary and produces initial block library. Ola1 blocks exhibit stable attractor dynamics with well-defined entropic signatures (mean participation entropy PE $\approx 0.66$, lock quality $>0.99$ for Grade A).

\item \textbf{Ola2 ($N = 2$--$5$):} Explorer proposes candidates with $L_{\min} = 0.75$, phase\_var $\leq 0.05$, quality\_lock $\geq 0.70$. Sweep integrates ODEs and computes observables.

\item \textbf{Ola3 ($N = 6$--$9$):} Tightened: $L_{\min} = 0.88$, phase\_var $\leq 0.03$, quality\_lock $\geq 0.85$.

\item \textbf{Ola4 ($N = 9$--$12$):} Strictest: $L_{\min} = 0.89$, phase\_var $\leq 0.02$, quality\_lock $\geq 0.89$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Crucially, Taxonomy classification is constant across all olas:} LOCKED requires quality\_lock $\geq 0.95$, $L \geq 0.90$, phase\_var $\leq 0.02$; Grade A requires $R \geq 0.60$ \emph{and} PE\_lock $\geq 0.12$; Grade B requires $R \geq 0.45$ \emph{and} PE\_lock $\geq 0.08$. Grades are assigned independently of the LOCKED label; promotion requires Grade A or B (the LOCKED classification is diagnostic but not a promotion gate).

Table~\ref{tab:funnel} shows the resulting survival funnel: promotion rates collapse from 100\% (Ola2) to 3.9\% (Ola3) to 0.7\% (Ola4). Grade A configurations disappear entirely at $N \geq 6$. This is not a pipeline artifact---it reflects the physical reality that high-quality coherence becomes sharply rare as complexity increases.

The conjunction of requirements (coherence \emph{and} entropy \emph{and} phase stability) makes spurious survival highly improbable: an entity cannot ``game'' the filter by excelling on one metric while failing others.

\section{Results}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Power-law coherence decay}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figure_1_Coherence_Scaling.png}
\caption{Coherence $R$ versus network size $N$. Points colored by pipeline stage (Ola2--4); black crosses show per-$N$ means. Dashed line: power-law fit $R \propto N^{-0.32}$. Alternative fits (inverse, exponential) achieve comparable goodness-of-fit; see Appendix~\ref{app:modelcomp}.}
\label{fig:scaling}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} shows global coherence $R$ versus $N$ across the full Ola ensemble. Over $N = 2$--$12$, the data are well-described by:
\begin{equation}
R(N) = R_0 \, N^{-\alpha}, \quad \alpha = 0.32 \pm 0.03
\end{equation}
with $R_0 \approx 0.83$. Alternative two-parameter forms (inverse, exponential) are not decisively excluded at this range (Appendix~\ref{app:modelcomp}), but linear decay is strongly disfavored.

\textbf{Robustness:} The only $N$ appearing in multiple olas is $N = 9$. A two-sample $t$-test shows no significant difference in $R$ between Ola3 and Ola4 at this $N$ ($p = 0.27$), confirming that scaling is intrinsic to network size, not a pipeline artifact.

\textbf{Physical interpretation:} Adding nodes incurs a ``coordination cost''---coherence degrades geometrically. A 10-node network has roughly half the mean coherence of a 3-node network.

\subsection{Memory as stabilization overhead}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figure_2_Memory_Overhead.png}
\caption{Memory participation (S2-share) versus $N$ for survivors (green) and failures (gray). At $N \leq 5$, no failures occur and S2-share is uniformly high. At $N \geq 7$, survivors cluster at elevated S2-share relative to failures, indicating memory recruitment for stability.}
\label{fig:memory}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:memory} shows S2-share versus $N$, split by outcome. Two regimes emerge:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low $N$ ($\leq 5$):} All configurations survive; S2-share is high and uniform. Geometry suffices for coordination.
\item \textbf{High $N$ ($\geq 7$):} Survivors maintain elevated S2-share ($\gtrsim 0.6$); failures scatter widely. Memory engagement distinguishes success from collapse.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Crucially, S2 does not \emph{cause} coherence.} Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows that within fixed $N$, the S2--$R$ relationship is nearly flat (slope $\approx -0.003 \pm 0.007$). Memory is \emph{overhead}---strongly associated with survival under geometric stress, but not sufficient to guarantee it.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figure_4_Mechanism_Binned.png}
\caption{Mechanism check: coherence residual ($R - \langle R|N\rangle$) versus S2-share. Binned means (blue) are statistically consistent with zero slope, ruling out S2 as a direct coherence driver.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Correlation shift:} Appendix~\ref{app:spearman} shows that the Spearman correlation between S2-share and $R$ shifts across $N$: negative at low $N$ ($\rho \approx -0.3$ to $-0.5$), near-zero or weakly positive at high $N$ ($\rho \approx 0$ to $+0.1$, not individually significant). The transition occurs around $N \approx 7$--$8$. 

To summarize the memory-coherence relationship: S2-share is predictive of survival (a selection effect---high S2 is strongly associated with promotion at large $N$), but conditional on survival, S2-share shows near-zero association with the level of coherence achieved.

\subsection{Topological ceiling and selectivity}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figure_3_Topology_Ceiling.png}
\caption{Survival rate (fraction with $R \geq 0.85$) by topology and $N$. Rings collapse sharply at $N \approx 6$--$8$; bipartites persist to $N = 10$--$12$ with degraded but nonzero coherence. Sample sizes shown in each cell.}
\label{fig:topology}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:topology} reveals strong topology dependence:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rings (monolithic):} High survival ($>90$\%) at $N = 2$--$4$; collapse to $<20$\% at $N = 6$--$8$; near-zero by $N = 9$.
\item \textbf{Bipartites (modular):} Maintain $40$--$90$\% survival even at $N = 10$--$12$. Compartmentalization reduces global coordination burden.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The ceiling:} Within the explored template family and parameter ranges, single-domain topologies show onset of collapse at $N \approx 6$--$7$ and reach near-zero survival (effective ceiling) by $N \approx 9$. This parallels the loss of single-domain Abrikosov order at high fields in vortex systems.

\textbf{Topology selectivity:} At low $N$, topology barely matters---many configurations work. At high $N$, topology is decisive. This parallels vortex phenomenology: at low fields, hexagonal, square, and disordered lattices all exist; at high fields, only certain geometries (domain-fragmented, anisotropic) retain residual order~\cite{Guillamon2009}.

\subsection{Critical heterogeneity at the transition}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figure5_Critical_Heterogeneity_Onset.png}
\caption{Interquartile range (IQR) of phase variance versus $N$. The spread increases by five orders of magnitude from $N = 2$ to $N = 6$--$7$, indicating coexistence of tightly locked and loosely metastable configurations at the transition.}
\label{fig:heterogeneity}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:heterogeneity} shows that phase-variance dispersion (IQR) peaks dramatically at $N = 6$--$7$---precisely the transition region identified above. This indicates \emph{critical heterogeneity}: different configurations explore qualitatively distinct dynamical basins near the structural limit.

\textbf{Vortex analog:} Guillamón et al.~\cite{Guillamon2009} observed ``breathing'' dynamics in NbSe$_2$ near the melting transition---vortices hopping between configurations with wide positional variance. Our phase-variance peak is the dynamical analog: internal structure ``dances'' at the boundary between ordered and disordered regimes.

\section{Discussion}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{What the results establish}

Our simulations demonstrate that a minimal network of delayed oscillators with internal memory exhibits:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A robust power-law coherence decay with exponent $\approx 1/3$;
\item A regime transition where memory shifts from overhead to enabling condition;
\item A topological ceiling for single-domain configurations;
\item Critical heterogeneity near the transition.
\end{enumerate}

These are \emph{intrinsic} properties of the dynamics---they emerge from the interplay of delay, memory, and topology, without reference to external data or material parameters.

\subsection{Theoretical context: parallels to disordered systems}

The correspondences to disordered condensed matter (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}) are numerically suggestive:

\begin{itemize}
\item The effective exponent $\alpha \approx 0.32$ falls within the range predicted by Larkin--Ovchinnikov collective-pinning theory for quasi-2D systems ($\alpha \sim 0.3$--$0.5$).
\item The S2 correlation shift is qualitatively consistent with the dual role of disorder near phase transitions.
\item The topological ceiling parallels loss of single-domain order at high complexity/field.
\end{itemize}

These correspondences motivate a \emph{phenomenological hypothesis}: DOFT networks may provide a minimal grammar for understanding coherence decay in systems where collective order competes with complexity-induced disorder. The vortex matter analogy is one concrete instantiation; other disordered systems might exhibit similar scaling.

\textbf{Quantitative test: disorder-energy scaling.} A more stringent test of the Bragg-glass correspondence is whether a suitably defined roughness-like proxy scales logarithmically with $N$. In Appendix~\ref{app:disorder_proxy}, we show that the memory-weighted proxy $E_{\mathrm{disorder}} = (1-R) \times H_{\mathrm{part}}$ satisfies $E_{\mathrm{disorder}} \propto \ln N$ with $R^2 = 0.97$, consistent with Bragg-glass roughness predictions for pinned elastic media~\cite{GiamarchiLeDoussal1994,Giamarchi1995}. This provides a quantitative bridge between the phenomenological mapping (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}) and theoretical predictions for disordered vortex lattices.

\subsection{What the results do \emph{not} establish}

We emphasize what we are \emph{not} claiming:

\begin{itemize}
\item We do not claim vortices ``are'' delayed oscillators.
\item We do not derive the $N \leftrightarrow H$ mapping from microscopic principles.
\item We do not claim the exponent is \emph{exactly} $1/3$ or universal across all variants.
\item We do not claim the model replaces BCS, Ginzburg--Landau, or collective-pinning theory.
\end{itemize}

The claim is \emph{phenomenological}: the mathematical structure of coherence loss in our model parallels that in vortex systems, making specific predictions testable.

\subsection{Falsification criteria}

The hypothesis that DOFT scaling parallels vortex matter fails if:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exponent mismatch:} Correlation-length measurements yield $\alpha < 0.2$ or $\alpha > 0.45$, extracted via comparable methodology (power-law fit over at least one decade).
\item \textbf{No role crossover:} Disorder/pinning consistently enhances or disrupts order across the entire field range, with no regime change.
\item \textbf{No topology selectivity:} Lattice symmetry remains degenerate at all fields.
\item \textbf{No ceiling:} Single-domain order persists to arbitrarily high fields.
\end{enumerate}

More broadly, the DOFT framework itself is falsified if independent implementations fail to reproduce $R \propto N^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha \in [0.25, 0.40]$ over the range $N = 2$--$12$.

\subsection{Proposed experimental tests}

\textbf{Test 1: Correlation-length scaling.} Perform $\mu$SR or SANS in layered superconductors (e.g., YBCO, BSCCO) in the intermediate-field regime below $H_{c2}$. Measure local field variance $\sigma_B(H)$ or Bragg width. Prediction: scaling exponent $\alpha \approx 0.30$--$0.35$.

\textbf{Test 2: Disorder role crossover.} Compare pristine versus irradiated samples. At low fields, disorder should reduce $\xi$; near the order-disorder transition, disorder may stabilize residual $\xi$.

\textbf{Test 3: Symmetry selectivity.} Use STM or decoration imaging at high fields. Prediction: residual order should fragment into domains or show anisotropic symmetry breaking.

\section{Conclusion}
%=============================================================================

We have characterized the intrinsic scaling of coherence, memory participation, and topological constraints in networks of coupled oscillators with internal memory (DOFT). The main findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Coherence is well-described by $R \propto N^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha \approx 0.32$, robust across topologies within the explored range $N = 2$--$12$.
\item Memory acts as stabilization overhead, with a correlation shift from negative to near-zero around $N \approx 7$--$8$.
\item Single-domain topologies hit a structural ceiling at $N \approx 8$--$9$; modular topologies survive longer with degraded coherence.
\item Promotion rates collapse from 100\% to 0.7\% as $N$ increases, quantifying the topological ceiling.
\end{enumerate}

These patterns exhibit suggestive parallels to correlation-length scaling in disordered condensed matter, particularly vortex matter in type-II superconductors. We propose these correspondences as \emph{falsifiable hypotheses}.

The DOFT framework, including source code and the complete numerical dataset, is publicly available. We encourage independent verification and extension of these results.

\section{Model Details}
\label{app:model}

The three-layer oscillator dynamics are governed by:
\begin{align}
\dot{Q}_1^{(j)} &= \omega_0 + K_Q \sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}(j)} \sin\bigl(Q_1^{(k)}(t-\tau) - Q_1^{(j)}\bigr) + \gamma_{QS} S_1^{(j)} \\
\dot{S}_1^{(j)} &= \gamma_{SQ}(Q_1^{(j)} - S_1^{(j)}) + \gamma_{SS} S_2^{(j)} \\
\dot{S}_2^{(j)} &= \gamma_{S2}(S_1^{(j)} - S_2^{(j)})
\end{align}
where $\omega_0$ is the natural frequency, $K_Q$ the coupling strength, $\tau$ the delay, and $\gamma_{XY}$ are relaxation rates. The S2 layer has the slowest relaxation ($\gamma_{S2} \ll \gamma_{SQ}$), providing memory.

\section{Model Comparison for Scaling Law}
\label{app:modelcomp}

To assess whether the power-law form $R = a N^{-\alpha}$ is uniquely supported, we compare four candidate models over the 11 per-$N$ mean values:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Model comparison for $R(N)$ scaling over $N = 2$--$12$.}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Form} & \textbf{AIC} & \textbf{BIC} & \textbf{$k$} \\
\midrule
Power law & $R = 0.83 \, N^{-0.33}$ & $-99.2$ & $-98.4$ & 2 \\
Inverse & $R = 0.72/N + 0.32$ & $-99.3$ & $-98.5$ & 2 \\
Exponential & $R = 0.58 \, e^{-0.34N} + 0.37$ & $-107.8$ & $-106.6$ & 3 \\
Linear & $R = -0.025N + 0.64$ & $-69.0$ & $-68.2$ & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\label{tab:modelcomp}
\end{table}

The exponential-with-offset form is statistically preferred by both AIC and BIC, even accounting for the additional parameter. The fitted offset ($R_\infty \approx 0.37$) suggests a nonzero asymptotic coherence, which may reflect the contribution of modular topologies that maintain residual order at large $N$.

However, over the available range ($N = 2$--$12$), the exponential is well-approximated by an effective power law with $\alpha \approx 0.32$. We report the power-law exponent because: (i) it provides a compact summary of the decay rate; (ii) it connects to theoretical predictions in disordered systems; (iii) discriminating between exponential and power-law asymptotic behavior requires extending measurements to larger $N$, which we leave to future work.

The honest conclusion is that coherence decays faster than linearly, with an effective exponent near $1/3$, but the precise functional form---and whether $R \to 0$ or $R \to R_\infty > 0$ as $N \to \infty$---remains underdetermined.

\textbf{Robustness check:} We verified the exponent estimate by refitting on all entity-level data points with cluster-robust standard errors (clustering by $N$); the result $\alpha = 0.32 \pm 0.04$ is consistent with the per-$N$ mean analysis.

\section{Pipeline Consistency Test}
\label{app:pipeline_test}

The only $N$ value appearing in multiple pipeline stages is $N = 9$ (both Ola3 and Ola4). If the scaling were a pipeline artifact (due to different thresholds or candidate pools), we would expect systematic differences in $R$ between olas at the same $N$.

\textbf{Result:} At $N = 9$, Ola3 yields $\langle R \rangle = 0.395 \pm 0.021$ ($n = 52$) and Ola4 yields $\langle R \rangle = 0.400 \pm 0.022$ ($n = 62$). A two-sample $t$-test gives $t = -1.12$, $p = 0.27$---no significant difference.

This confirms that the coherence scaling is intrinsic to network size, not an artifact of the pipeline stage or threshold selection.

\section{Memory-Weighted Disorder Proxy}
\label{app:disorder_proxy}

The mapping between DOFT networks and vortex phenomenology (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}) suggests that a disorder-related proxy should scale with network complexity. In Bragg-glass theory, roughness and displacement correlators grow logarithmically with the ratio of system size to correlation length~\cite{GiamarchiLeDoussal1994,Giamarchi1995}:
\begin{equation}
\langle u^2 \rangle \sim \ln(r/\ell)
\label{eq:roughness}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is a characteristic scale, $\ell$ the lattice constant, and $u$ the displacement field. We construct an analogous roughness-like proxy for DOFT networks.

\textbf{Naive proxy fails.} A first attempt using phase variance alone (phase\_var\_lastW) shows poor correlation with $\ln N$ ($R^2 = 0.08$; Figure~\ref{fig:proxy_comparison}a). This is expected: phase variance captures instantaneous spread but not the interplay between geometric order and memory stabilization.

\textbf{Memory-weighted proxy.} We define a composite disorder proxy that multiplicatively couples coherence loss with entropic participation:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{disorder}} = (1 - R) \times H_{\mathrm{part}}
\label{eq:E_disorder}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the global coherence (R\_network\_S1\_mean) and $H_{\mathrm{part}}$ is the normalized partition entropy (H\_part\_norm\_mean). This form captures the intuition that disorder energy reflects both the failure of geometric coordination ($1-R$) and the entropic cost of maintaining residual order ($H_{\mathrm{part}}$).

\textbf{Result.} Aggregating by network size $N$ (11 bins, $N = 2$--$12$) and fitting the bin means via ordinary least squares against $\ln N$ yields:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{disorder}} \approx 0.12 \cdot \ln N + 0.07, \quad R^2 = 0.97
\label{eq:E_fit}
\end{equation}
(Figure~\ref{fig:proxy_comparison}b). The logarithmic scaling is consistent with Bragg-glass predictions under the identification $N \leftrightarrow r/\ell$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{memory_weighted_proxy_comparation.png}
\caption{Comparison of disorder proxies versus network complexity $\ln N$. 
\textbf{(a)}~Phase variance alone shows poor correlation ($R^2 = 0.08$). 
\textbf{(b)}~The memory-weighted proxy $E_{\mathrm{disorder}} = (1-R) \times H_{\mathrm{part}}$ exhibits robust logarithmic scaling ($R^2 = 0.97$), consistent with Bragg-glass theory predictions. 
Error bars indicate standard error of the mean across entities within each $N$ bin.}
\label{fig:proxy_comparison}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Physical interpretation.} The multiplicative form $(1-R) \times H_{\mathrm{part}}$ is not arbitrary. At low $N$, coherence is high ($R \to 1$), so $(1-R) \to 0$ regardless of entropy---geometric order dominates. At high $N$, coherence degrades ($R \to 0.4$), and the entropic term $H_{\mathrm{part}}$ modulates how much ``disorder energy'' is required to maintain residual coordination. The product captures this crossover naturally.

\textbf{Stability proxy.} Inverting the disorder energy defines a topological stability measure:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{topo}} = \frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{disorder}}} = \frac{1}{(1-R) \times H_{\mathrm{part}}}
\label{eq:S_topo}
\end{equation}
which decreases monotonically with $N$ and correlates with survival probability. Entities with anomalously high $S_{\mathrm{topo}}$ at large $N$ tend to be survivors, suggesting this metric captures the ``cost of coherence'' that distinguishes promoted configurations.

\textbf{Connection to vortex phenomenology.} In type-II superconductors, the peak effect---an anomalous increase in critical current near $H_{c2}$---is associated with softening of the vortex lattice and enhanced pinning~\cite{Paltiel2000}. The position of this peak typically occurs at $H/H_{c2} \approx 0.7$--$0.9$ depending on material and disorder~\cite{Fasano2005}. 

In our data, a qualitative analog appears: survival rates show a local minimum at $N \approx 9$ followed by a slight rebound at $N = 10$--$12$ for certain topologies (bipartites). However, the statistical significance is marginal (based on 5--7 survivors at $N \geq 10$), and we present this observation as preliminary rather than established. The correspondence $N/N_{\max} = 10/12 \approx 0.83$ versus $H/H_{c2} \approx 0.8$ is suggestive but requires larger samples to confirm.

\textbf{Limitations.} The mapping $N \leftrightarrow r/\ell$ is phenomenological, not derived. Network node count measures topological complexity, not spatial distance. The logarithmic scaling is empirical over one decade ($N = 2$--$12$); extension to larger $N$ is needed to test asymptotic behavior. We report this proxy as a \emph{quantitative tool} consistent with vortex phenomenology, not as proof of microscopic equivalence.
